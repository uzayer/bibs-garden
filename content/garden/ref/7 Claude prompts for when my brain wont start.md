01

The Brain-Dump Sorter The prompt: "My head is full of open loops and I can't think straight. I'm going to dump every task, worry, and half-thought below. Sort them into three buckets: Do Now, Do Later, and Let Go. For the 'Do Now' bucket, give me only the top 3, ranked, plus one concrete first action for each. Don't comment on anything in the other two buckets.

Why it works: Getting the loops out of your head and onto the screen stops the mental looping that eats your focus. Capping "Do Now" at three is the upgrade most people miss, otherwise the sorted list just becomes a new pile to feel guilty about.

02 The First-Move Unlocker

The prompt: "I'm staring at [task] and physically can't start. Break it into steps so small that each one takes under 60 seconds. Then show me only the first step, and make it something I do with my hands (open the doc, type one line, grab the folder). Hold the rest of the list back until I say 'next.'" +

Why it works: Task paralysis usually isn't laziness, it's not knowing the literal first move. A physical, sub-minute action is small enough to slip past the resistance, and hiding the full list keeps it from re- triggering the freeze the second you start.

03

The Focus-Fuel Menu The prompt: "I'm under-stimulated and drifting. Build me a 'focus menu using only things I can do right now at [home / my desk / wherever | am]: three 5-minute 'snacks' (quick movement or a fast win), three 25-minute 'mains' (real deep work), and three 10-minute 'sides' (something creative or fun). Be specific to my situation, no generic advice." +

Why it works: Time blindness comes from only picturing the core task, never the hidden glue around it. Surfacing the forgotten sub-steps and handing you a range instead of one optimistic number is what makes the deadline survivable.

04

The Real-Deadline Calculator The prompt:

"I think [task] will take [your guess]. I'm almost always wrong about this. Walk me through the steps I'm forgetting (setup, switching, reviewing, the 'oh wait' moments), then give me three estimates: best case, realistic, worst case. Add a buffer and tell me the block of time I should actually put on the calendar."

Why it works: Time blindness comes from only picturing the core task, never the hidden glue around it. Surfacing the forgotten sub-steps and handing you a range instead of one optimistic number is what makes the deadline survivable.
